Paul is going to answer the question all of us ask on our worst days: can anything separate me from God's love? By the end of this episode you'll have three reasons, straight from the text, to sleep well even when life isn't going well.

Let's pray. Father, thank you for Romans 8. Give us ears to hear what is already written and a heart that rests in it. In Jesus' name, amen.

## Where we are in Romans 8
A quick reminder for anyone joining for the first time today. Romans 8 opens with "there is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus" and closes with nothing being "able to separate us from the love of God" in verse 39. Between those two lines is the whole chapter. It starts with no condemnation and ends with no separation. And what's in between is the believer's life: the Spirit, suffering, hope, prayer.

Today we're reading verses 28 through 39. That's twelve verses and we're going slowly, because there are lines here people repeat without knowing where they come from or what they actually promise. And there are others people avoid because they sound hard, like predestinate. We're not skipping any of them.

## First reason: God is working in everything, though not in everything the same way
[TEXTO: Romans 8:28]
Verse 28 says: "And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose."

This is one of the most quoted verses in the Bible and one of the most misunderstood. Notice what it doesn't say. It doesn't claim that all things are good, or that everything that happens to you is good. It says all things work together for good. They cooperate. They work toward an end.

Think of a recipe. Raw flour isn't good. Salt on its own isn't good. An oven at four hundred degrees isn't good if you put your hand in it. But together, in the right order and with the right timing, they make bread. Paul doesn't say the oven is pleasant. He says the baker knows what he's doing.

Notice the first words too: we know. Not we feel. Not we hope. We know. This is something the believer knows even when they don't feel it, the way you know the sun is still there on a cloudy day. Paul isn't describing an emotion; he's describing a fact that the emotion takes a while to catch up with.

And who is this promise for? Them that love God, them who are the called according to his purpose. This isn't a universal promise that everything works out for everyone. It's a promise for those inside the relationship. That doesn't make it smaller; it makes it more secure, because it rests on God's purpose and not on luck.

And what is that good everything cooperates toward? Don't guess. Paul defines it in the next verse.

[TEXTO: Romans 8:29-30]
"For whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be the firstborn among many brethren. Moreover whom he did predestinate, them he also called: and whom he called, them he also justified: and whom he justified, them he also glorified."

The good is to be conformed to the image of his Son. To look like Jesus. That is where the recipe ends. So when something painful happens and you ask what is this for, the answer of Romans 8 isn't so you can have a bigger house. It's so you look more like Christ. Sometimes that hurts, because there are parts of us that look nothing like him and have to come out.

Now, the scary word: predestinate. Sincere Christians have spent centuries arguing how this fits with human freedom, and we're not going to settle that in twenty minutes. But notice what Paul uses the word for here. He doesn't use it so you'll argue. He uses it so you'll rest. The point of the passage is that your salvation started with God's decision, not yours, and so it doesn't end with your failures. If you had started it, you could ruin it. Since he started it, he finishes it.

And look at the chain in verse 30. Foreknew, predestinated, called, justified, glorified. Five links. And something very strange: all of them are in the past tense. Even glorified. But we haven't been glorified yet; we're still here, with bad backs and bills to pay. Why does Paul put it in the past?

Because for God the chain is already complete. What he starts, he finishes. Justified means he declared you righteous, innocent, because of what Christ did. Glorified means the end of the road: when we are like him, without sin and with a new body. And if he justified you, glorification is so certain that Paul writes it as if it had already happened. Here is the first reason to sleep well: God doesn't leave chains half-finished.

Have you ever felt God let go of you halfway? Look at verse 30 again. There's no link where it says some of them. It says them also. The same ones. Those who go in one end come out the other. Nobody gets lost between links.

## Second reason: the only one who could condemn you is the one who died for you
[TEXTO: Romans 8:31-34]
Now Paul changes tone. He stops explaining and starts asking. Reading 31 through 34: "What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us? He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things? Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect? It is God that justifieth. Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died, yea rather, that is risen again, who is even at the right hand of God, who also maketh intercession for us."

Four questions in a row. Paul writes like a lawyer in a courtroom. And the first is the one everybody quotes: if God be for us, who can be against us? But be careful, because that line doesn't say nobody is against you. There will be people against you, illness against you, circumstances against you. It says, instead, that none of them weighs as much as God for you.

Then the argument of verse 32, one of the strongest pieces of reasoning in the whole Bible. It goes from greater to lesser. If God already gave you the most expensive thing he had, his own Son, is he going to deny you the cheap things? If someone gives you a house, they're not going to withhold the keys. He that spared not the Son will not spare the rest.

There's an echo here Paul wants you to hear. Spared not his own Son. That's the phrase the angel said to Abraham in Genesis 22 when he stopped his hand over Isaac: because thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son. God stopped Abraham. He did not stop himself. What he spared Abraham, God paid in full. When you doubt whether God is for you, look at that mountain.

Now, the truth is the question that reaches me most is the one in verse 33. Who shall lay anything to the charge? Because I know who accuses me. My memory accuses me, my conscience accuses me, the enemy accuses me, sometimes people who know me accuse me. And Paul answers with one line: it is God that justifieth. The judge already gave the verdict. The accusations can keep coming, but they arrive after the sentence, and the sentence was innocent.

[B-ROLL: empty courtroom, judge's gavel on the bench]
And verse 34 is even better. Who is he that condemneth? The only person with authority to condemn you is Christ. And look at what Christ is doing according to Paul: died, risen, at the right hand of God, making intercession for us. Four things, and all four in your favor. And the one person who could condemn you is busy praying for you. Here is the second reason to sleep well.

Think about this for a moment. When you go to bed accusing yourself over what you did today, at that very moment Christ is before the Father speaking well of you. Not defending what you did, but presenting what he did for you. You accuse yourself at night; he defends you at night. And his voice carries farther than yours.

## Third reason: Paul's list is longer than yours
[TEXTO: Romans 8:35-39]
And we come to the end. I'm going to read verses 35 through 39 in full and then walk through them. If you have your Bible, follow along with the text on screen. Romans 8:35-39:

"Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword? As it is written, For thy sake we are killed all the day long; we are accounted as sheep for the slaughter. Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us. For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."

[PAUSA]

Paul makes two lists. The first, in verse 35, is things that happen in life: tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword. Seven. And notice these are things Paul lived. He isn't speaking in theory. He went hungry, he was persecuted, and the sword came for him in the end. This isn't a comfortable pastor telling everyone else to trust.

And between the two lists he quotes Psalm 44: we are accounted as sheep for the slaughter. Why put that there? Because he wants you to know the believer's suffering is not a surprise or a sign God left. It's been in the plan since the Psalms. God's people have always suffered, and God's love has always been there in the middle of the suffering. Both truths fit in the same sentence.

Then he says the phrase: more than conquerors. In Greek it's one word, hypernikomen, and it means to win with room to spare, to win with an advantage. It's not surviving by a hair. It's that the thing that came to destroy you ends up serving the purpose of verse 28. Okay, that's what being more than a conqueror is: the enemy's weapon ends up working for you.

Let me give you an example. Joseph, in Genesis. His brothers sell him, he's falsely accused, he's forgotten in prison. Thirteen years of that. And at the end he tells them: ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good. Joseph doesn't justify what they did. He affirms that God steered it. Same as Romans 8:28, but with a name attached. Joseph didn't just survive his brothers: his brothers' betrayal was the road by which God saved the whole family. Joseph was more than a conqueror.

The second list, in verses 38 and 39, isn't things in life anymore; it's powers. Death, life, angels, principalities, powers, present, future, height, depth. Paul is covering every possible dimension: time, space, the visible world and the invisible one. And in case he missed something, he closes with nor any other creature.

Notice he puts death first. For most people death is the final separation, the one that cuts everything. Paul puts it first on the list of things that cannot separate you. And then he puts life, because there are days when life separates more than death does: routine, exhaustion, distraction. Neither one nor the other.

Now think about this with me. What is the one thing not on the list of created things? The Creator. So the one being who could separate you from God's love is God. And God just told you, in the previous verses, that he is for you, that he spared not his Son and that he already justified you. Paul's list is longer than any list of fears you could write tonight. That's the third reason.

Do you realize you're on that list too? You are a created thing. Not even you can separate yourself from God's love in Christ. That isn't a license to live however you want; Romans 6 already closed that door. It's the assurance that when you fail, God's love doesn't fail with you.

## What this passage does not promise
Before we close I want to be honest about something, because this passage is often misused. Romans 8 doesn't promise you won't suffer. The opposite: the list in verse 35 assumes you will. It doesn't promise you'll understand why. Joseph took thirteen years to understand, and there are things we'll only understand in glory. And it doesn't promise you'll feel God's love every day. Paul says I am persuaded, not I am excited.

Its promise is more solid than all of that. It promises that, whatever happens, God's love in Christ does not move. That the chain is complete. That the Judge has spoken. And that the list is longer than yours. If you don't feel it today, that's all right. Paul didn't write it from a feeling either. He wrote it from a fact.

## What we do with this
Today we saw three reasons to sleep well. God doesn't leave chains half-finished: foreknew, predestinated, called, justified, glorified. The only one who could condemn you is interceding for you. And Paul's list of what can't separate you is longer than your list of fears.

The big idea today is this: the believer's security is not in what they feel, but in what God has already done and will not undo.

Read them facing each other every night before you sleep. Not to deny the first list, but to put it in its place.
